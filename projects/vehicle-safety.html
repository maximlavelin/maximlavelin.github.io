Vehicle Safety Intelligence: Classifying NHTSA Complaints at Scale

Result: Trained and rigorously compared MLP / CNN / BiLSTM architectures on ~300,000 NHTSA vehicle complaints, reaching an ROC-AUC of ≈ 0.97 [confirm vs. report] on held-out test data, with calibration and bootstrap confidence intervals reported alongside every metric — not just a single point estimate.

The problem

NHTSA receives hundreds of thousands of free-text vehicle safety complaints. Manually triaging them for severity or defect category doesn't scale. This project builds a text classification pipeline to automatically flag and categorize complaints, comparing progressively more expressive model architectures under a rigorous, statistically honest evaluation protocol.

Data & preprocessing

  • ~300,000 free-text NHTSA complaint records
  • Text cleaning, tokenization, and TF-IDF vectorization as a classical baseline representation
  • [Add: target variable definition — e.g. severity class, defect category, or injury-flag]

Approach

Three architectures of increasing complexity, to test whether deep sequence models actually earn their added cost over simpler baselines on this task:

  • MLP on TF-IDF features — a fast, interpretable baseline
  • CNN — captures local n-gram-like patterns in complaint text
  • BiLSTM — models sequential/contextual structure across the full complaint

Evaluation — beyond accuracy

Rather than reporting a single accuracy number, evaluation included:

  • ROC and Precision-Recall curves — important given class imbalance in complaint severity
  • Calibration analysis — checking whether predicted probabilities are trustworthy, not just whether the top class is correct
  • Bootstrap confidence intervals — quantifying how much metrics would vary on a different sample, rather than trusting one train/test split
