Transit Gap: A Spatial Accessibility Index for Public Transport

GIS & AIPython (Colab)CBS Census 2022 + GTFS
Built a 400-metre accessibility and need index combining CBS Census 2022 with GTFS transit data, using Getis-Ord Gi* to identify statistically significant transit deserts — exported as GeoJSON and CSV ready for direct use in ArcGIS.
PythonGeoPandasShapelyGTFSSpatial joinsGetis-Ord Gi*GeoJSON
Full report (PDF)Notebook (.ipynb)

Problem

Transit planning needs a precise, defensible definition of “underserved” rather than a visual impression of a sparse map. This project builds a quantitative accessibility index from population data and real transit stop locations, then tests which gaps are statistically significant rather than incidental.

Data

  • CBS Census 2022 — population and demographic data at statistical-area level.
  • GTFS feeds — actual transit stop locations and service schedules.

Approach

  • Built 400-metre walking buffers around every transit stop using GeoPandas geometry operations.
  • Spatial joins between population polygons and buffer coverage, to compute what share of each area's population has walkable access.
  • Constructed an accessibility and need index combining coverage with population density and [demographic factors, e.g. age or car-ownership].
  • Applied Getis-Ord Gi* hot-spot analysis, which identifies clusters of high or low accessibility unlikely to arise by chance — the step that turns a map into a finding.
  • Exported GeoJSON and CSV structured for direct import into ArcGIS, so a planner can use the output without touching the code.

Results

[Summarise: identified [N] statistically significant transit-desert clusters, concentrated in [region type], covering roughly [X]% of the study area's population.]

Limitations

[For example: the index treats stop presence as binary, but a stop served once an hour is not meaningfully accessible — incorporating service frequency and wait time would sharpen it, as would validating against reported commute times.]
