Transit Gap: A Spatial Accessibility Index for Public Transport

Result: Built a 400m-buffer accessibility/need index combining CBS Census 2022 and GTFS transit data, using Getis-Ord Gi* to identify statistically significant "transit deserts" — exported as GeoJSON/CSV ready for direct use in ArcGIS.

The problem

Transit planning decisions need to identify which neighborhoods are underserved — but "underserved" needs a precise, defensible definition, not a gut feeling. This project builds a quantitative accessibility index combining population data with actual transit stop locations and schedules, to flag areas that are statistically significant gaps rather than just visually sparse on a map.

Data

  • CBS Census 2022 — population and demographic data at the statistical area level
  • GTFS (General Transit Feed Specification) — real transit stop locations and schedules

Approach

  • Built 400m walking-distance buffers around every transit stop using GeoPandas spatial operations
  • Spatial joins between population data and buffer coverage to compute what share of each area's population has walkable transit access
  • Constructed an accessibility/need index combining population density, transit coverage, and [demographic factors, e.g. age/mobility-relevant population]
  • Applied Getis-Ord Gi* hot-spot analysis — a spatial statistics test that identifies clusters of high or low accessibility that are unlikely to be due to chance, not just areas that happen to look sparse
  • Exported results as GeoJSON/CSV, structured for direct import into ArcGIS — built for planners to use, not just to visualize in a notebook
