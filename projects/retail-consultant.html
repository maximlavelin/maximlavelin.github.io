Problem

Retailers sitting on raw transaction logs often cannot answer four basic questions: who are our best customers, what should we cross-sell, which accounts look anomalous, and what should we recommend next. This project builds one pipeline that answers all four, on the Online Retail II dataset from a UK online retailer.

Approach

  • RFM feature engineering — recency, frequency and monetary value per customer, the standard basis for customer value segmentation.
  • Clustering — K-Means compared against hierarchical agglomerative clustering, selecting on both silhouette score and whether the resulting groups were actually interpretable as customer types.
  • Apriori association rules — surfaced product bundles frequently bought together, directly usable for merchandising decisions.
  • Time-series decomposition — separated sales into trend, seasonality and residual to distinguish real demand shifts from noise.
  • Local Outlier Factor — flagged customers and transactions with unusual behaviour, relevant as fraud or churn-risk signals.
  • SVD recommender — matrix factorisation over the customer-product interaction matrix for personalised recommendations.

Limitations

With more time I would validate the segments against downstream outcomes such as repeat purchase rate and churn, rather than clustering metrics alone, and test DBSCAN as a density-based alternative for customer groups that are not cleanly spherical.
