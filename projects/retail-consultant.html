Retail Consultant: A Full Customer Analytics Pipeline

Result: Segmented customers into [N] actionable groups via RFM + clustering, mined association rules for cross-sell opportunities, flagged anomalous accounts, and built an SVD-based recommender — a single pipeline covering segmentation, merchandising, and retention on the Online Retail II dataset.

The problem

Retailers sitting on raw transaction logs often can't answer basic questions: who are our best customers, what should we cross-sell, which accounts look anomalous, and what should we recommend next? This project builds one pipeline that answers all four, using the Online Retail II dataset (UK-based online retailer, ~1M transactions).

Approach

  • RFM feature engineering — Recency, Frequency, Monetary value per customer, the standard basis for customer value segmentation
  • Clustering — compared K-Means against Hierarchical Agglomerative Clustering (HAC) to segment customers, selecting the approach that gave more interpretable, well-separated groups (silhouette score [X.XX])
  • Apriori association rule mining — surfaced product bundles frequently bought together, directly usable for cross-sell/merchandising decisions
  • Time-series decomposition — separated overall sales into trend, seasonality, and residual to distinguish real demand shifts from noise
  • LOF (Local Outlier Factor) anomaly detection — flagged customers/transactions with unusual behavior, relevant for fraud or churn-risk signals
  • SVD-based recommender system — matrix factorization over the customer-product interaction matrix to generate personalized recommendations

What I'd do differently

With more time, I'd validate the discovered segments against actual downstream outcomes (repeat purchase rate, churn) rather than proxy clustering metrics alone, and test DBSCAN as a density-based alternative for customer groups that aren't cleanly spherical.
