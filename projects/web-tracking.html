Web Tracking, Privacy & AI Profiling

The problem

"Websites track you" is a widely repeated claim that's rarely backed by structured evidence. This project asks: can we actually quantify and categorize tracking behavior across the web, and does the resulting picture support or complicate common assumptions about surveillance and AI-driven profiling?

Data & approach

  • WhoTracks.me dataset — tracking behavior across ~10,000 websites
  • Data cleaning and feature engineering on tracker counts, categories, and behaviors per site
  • StandardScaler normalization ahead of distance-based clustering
  • K-Means and DBSCAN compared — K-Means for a fixed number of interpretable tracking-behavior profiles, DBSCAN to check whether density-based structure reveals outlier sites (unusually aggressive or unusually clean trackers) that K-Means would average away
  • Statistical analysis to test whether differences between site categories (e.g. news vs. e-commerce vs. [other]) are significant, not just visually apparent

Why this matters beyond the numbers

This project is deliberately paired with an ethics framing: clustering isn't just a technical exercise here, it's evidence for a real policy/privacy question. That combination — technical rigor plus the judgment to reason about downstream impact — is exactly what's needed when building AI systems that touch real people's data.
